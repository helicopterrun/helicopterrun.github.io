People (Who's There)

People is the app's view of the faces your Marcellus sidecar has recognized. It is not part of plain Frigate, and it appears only when the server says it supports it: the sidecar must have face recognition enabled. If the server does not offer it, the app shows nothing for it, with no empty page.

The recognizing is done by your server. Faces, face images and the numbers used to match them stay on that server. The privacy policy has the full account.

IDs and names

Every face the sidecar groups together gets a permanent number, shown as #11. A name is an optional label on that number. You never need a name to see, follow, search or correct a face; an unnamed face is simply #11.

IDs are never reused. When two IDs turn out to be the same person and you merge them, the old number keeps working and points at the survivor.

An unnamed face is shown by its handle, a generated label such as "Shoal Gadwall". The handle is worked out from the ID number, never changes and is different for every ID. It is a label for you to talk about the face with, not a name: it does not change how the face is grouped, and it is not written to Frigate or to alerts. Where the app names a recognized person who has no name, such as in an encounter title, it uses the handle; your server works that out when the app asks and does not store it in the encounter.

Naming a face is different. When you name a person, your server writes that name into Frigate as the sub-label on their events, including earlier events that are still in Frigate, and encounter titles can use it. The name shows inside the app, which fetches it from your own server. It is not put in alerts (see Privacy below).

Finding People

  • iPhone and iPad: a People strip of faces sits at the top of the Search tab; tap one to open it, or open the full list from the strip.
  • Mac: People is in the sidebar.

The list groups faces as:

  • Today: recognized today.
  • Following: faces you follow.
  • Who is this?: faces with no name that were recognized on two or more days. These are the ones most worth naming.
  • Named: faces you have named.
  • Seen once: faces with no name recognized on a single day.

On iPhone and iPad the strip at the top of Search shows the people you follow, then anyone recognized today (on a quiet day with neither, the most recently seen faces), with an All button.

Faces also show up beside the names of recognized people elsewhere in the app: on the reel's markers and the event card under the player, in Search rows, and on cards in the Review tab. They sit beside the text, never over a picture, and appear only when your server knows who was in the event.

A person's page

The page shows a best crop, when the face was Last recognized and on which camera, how many days and sightings it covers, a Rhythm summary of the parts of the day and week they usually show up (once there are enough sightings), and their visits grouped by day as strips of face crops. Tap a visit to open that footage in Scrub, and Previous and Next then move between that person's visits across cameras. A Recent encounters row opens the Review feed for them (your server keeps encounters for 30 days).

Last recognized is the last time your server matched a face in a camera event to this person. It is a statement about the server's recognizing, not about where anyone is. The wording is deliberate: "Last recognized", never "last here". A person can be at your door and not appear here. Your server can only match a face the camera saw clearly enough: facing the lens, big enough, sharp enough, on a camera that is set up for face recognition, with the server running. Someone who keeps their back to the camera, wears a hat, or walks past a camera that is not enrolled is not recognized, whether or not they were there. So an old "Last recognized" is not evidence that someone has stayed away, and a missing face is not evidence that no one came.

The Actions menu on the page offers:

  • Name (or Rename).
  • Follow or Unfollow. A followed face moves to Following, shows in the strip at the top of Search, and is kept past the expiry described below.
  • Add note, up to 200 characters, for yourself ("probably the courier").
  • Merge: everything moves to the face you keep, and the other ID keeps pointing at it so old links still work. If both have different names, rename one first.
  • Delete, described below.

Within a visit you can also mark a sighting as Not this person, which removes just that sighting from them; the page offers an undo.

When unnamed faces are forgotten

An ID with no name or note that you do not follow, and that has not been recognized for 60 days, is forgotten: its ID, matching numbers and face images are deleted from your server. Anyone you have named, followed or given a note is kept until you delete them. The 60 days is your server's default; whoever runs the server can change it.

Deleting a person

Open the person, open the menu, and choose Delete. Your server then erases their face images, matching numbers and ID (and the "not the same" and accuracy-check answers that mention them), removes their name from your server's encounters (unless another person has the same name), and asks Frigate to clear the sub-label it wrote on their events. The Frigate step is best-effort: if Frigate cannot be reached at that moment, some sub-labels may need clearing in Frigate itself. Deleting cannot recall alerts you have already received. A deleted ID is never reused, and an old link to it shows that the person is no longer on your server.

To stop recognizing altogether, turn face recognition off in the Marcellus configuration. That stops new recognizing; delete the people you want gone to remove what is already stored.

Merge suggestions

When two IDs look like one person, a Might be the same person row appears at the top of the list. Each suggestion shows the faces side by side. Merge combines them (and fails if both already have different names); you can also say they are not the same, and that pair is not suggested again. Splitting one person into several IDs is the more common fault, so this is how existing IDs get tidied up. Compare faces shows each face large before you merge.

Review tab filter

On the Review tab, the Person button in the toolbar narrows the encounters to one person. It clears when you relaunch the app. A person's page also has a Recent encounters row that opens the same feed for them. Review needs the sidecar's encounters; see Search and Review.

Privacy

Face crops, IDs, names and recognition data stay on your server, and the app does not upload faces anywhere. Names, handles and face IDs are not included in push notifications and never reach the push relay or Apple's push service: an alert about a recognized person says "Person", like any other. Recognition can still make an alert quieter, but it does not change its words. See the privacy policy.
